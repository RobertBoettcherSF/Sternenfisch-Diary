# 2022-06-03 — Bardo Thodol, El mundo de ayer, Tao Te King, and The world of yesterday

## Bardo Thodol

**Date:** 2022-06-03

**Title:** Bardo Thodol

**Author:** Walter Evans-Wentz

**Themes:** Death, Passage, Liberation

## Synopsis: Bardo Thodol — Walter Evans-Wentz

Bardo Thodol belongs to the Tibetan Book of the Dead, a guide read for the dying and the dead through the interval between lives. The text describes visions, peaceful and wrathful, and repeats that they are projections of the mind.

The practical aim is recognition. If the dead person sees the appearances for what they are, release is possible; if not, another birth follows.

Editions and commentaries differ. The core remains a set of instructions for the hours when ordinary identity fails.

## El mundo de ayer

**Date:** 2022-06-03

**Title:** El mundo de ayer

**Author:** Stefan Zweig

**Themes:** Memory, Europe, Exile

## Synopsis: El mundo de ayer — Stefan Zweig

El mundo de ayer is Stefan Zweig's memoir of the Europe he lost, under this edition's title. The early pages are a grateful inventory of a cultured life. The later pages are the inventory's destruction.

War, dictatorship, and statelessness arrive as personal facts, not only as headlines. He writes from after the end.

The book is a farewell to a way of being a European, offered by someone who could not keep it.

## Tao Te King

**Date:** 2022-06-03

**Title:** Tao Te King

**Author:** Lao Tzu

**Themes:** Way, Simplicity, Paradox

## Synopsis: Tao Te King — Lao Tzu

Tao Te King is the short classic attributed to Lao Tzu, a sequence of poems and sayings about the Way that cannot be forced. Water, the valley, the uncarved block, and the ruler who governs by not shoving are recurring images.

The counsel is often a reversal: softness outlasts hardness, and the useful part of a cup is the empty space.

It is a book people return to a few verses at a time, because the sentences refuse to be used up.

## The world of yesterday

**Date:** 2022-06-03

**Title:** The world of yesterday

**Author:** Stefan Zweig

**Themes:** Memory, Europe, Exile

## Synopsis: The world of yesterday — Stefan Zweig

The world of yesterday is Stefan Zweig's memoir of the European culture he grew up in and then watched come apart. Vienna before 1914, the habits of reading and travel, and the friendships of a literary life fill the early pages.

The later chapters follow war, inflation, the rise of dictatorship, and the loss of a passport that once opened borders. Zweig writes as a man already outside the world he is describing.

It is a book about a civilization remembered from exile, not a chronicle of battles. The tone is grateful and bereaved at once.
