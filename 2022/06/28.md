**Date:** 2022-06-28

**Title:** Conversations

**Author:** Kong Fuzi

**Themes:** Conduct, Ritual, Government

## Synopsis: Conversations — Kong Fuzi

Conversations presents the conversations of Kong Fuzi, Confucius, with his students: short exchanges on learning, filial duty, ritual, and how a person should hold office. The form is sayings, not a continuous argument.

Character is trained in small courtesies and in the refusal to pretend. Government, in these pages, begins with the person who governs himself.

The book has been a schoolroom for centuries because the sentences are brief enough to live with.

**Title:** God and the State

**Author:** Mikhail Bakunin

**Themes:** Anarchism, Authority, Freedom

## Synopsis: God and the State — Mikhail Bakunin

God and the State is Mikhail Bakunin's attack on church and state as twin authorities over a free life. He argues that a divine master and a political one train the same habit of obedience.

The prose is polemical and unfinished in the way of a fighter's essay. Liberty, for him, is incompatible with a permanent ruler, sacred or secular.

It is a founding anarchist text, short and hot, not a constitution.

**Title:** Poor People

**Author:** Fyodor Mikhailovich Dostoevsky

**Themes:** Poverty, Letters, Petersburg

## Synopsis: Poor People — Fyodor Mikhailovich Dostoevsky

Poor People is Dostoevsky's first novel, told entirely in letters between a poor clerk and a young woman in the same Petersburg building. Makar Devushkin spends money he does not have to protect Varvara, and the letters record the dignity and the panic of that effort.

There is no detective plot. The drama is rent, a new dress, a dangerous offer from a richer man, and the way affection talks when it is ashamed.

The book asks what goodness is worth when it cannot pay the bill.

**Title:** The Cossacks

**Author:** Leo Tolstoy

**Themes:** Caucasus, Youth, Freedom

## Synopsis: The Cossacks — Leo Tolstoy

The Cossacks sends a young Russian officer, Olenin, to a Cossack village in the Caucasus, where he hopes to escape a stale Moscow life. Hunting, a local woman, and the example of a fighter who does not need his ideas complicate the hope.

Tolstoy is already suspicious of a tourist's longing to become 'natural.' The landscape is precise and the romance does not flatter the newcomer.

It is a short novel about the fantasy of starting over among people who were already at home.

**Title:** The Double

**Author:** Fyodor Mikhailovich Dostoevsky

**Themes:** Identity, Clerk, Doppelgänger

## Synopsis: The Double — Fyodor Mikhailovich Dostoevsky

The Double is Dostoevsky's story of a minor official who meets another man with his face and his name, a double who is better at the life he is failing. Office politics turn into a pursuit through the streets.

The comedy is anxious. Golyadkin's attempts to explain himself only make him look more replaceable.

It is an early, nightmarish study of a self that cannot hold its place.

**Title:** Die Spielhölle von Baden-Baden: Trente et Quarante

**Author:** Edmond About

**Themes:** Gambling, Risk, Society

## Synopsis: Die Spielhölle von Baden-Baden: Trente et Quarante — Edmond About

Die Spielhölle von Baden-Baden: Trente et Quarante names the gambling hell of Baden-Baden and the card game Trente et Quarante. Edmond About aims at the casino as a social machine: elegance, ruin, and the comedy of people who think the table is a personality test.

The setting is the spa town where nineteenth-century Europe went to lose money politely. The game in the subtitle is not decoration. It is the ritual.

Expect satire of risk and respectability in the same room.
