# 2022-08-26 — Entonces en casa, Hoy en casa - Otro libro -, Peasants, bigwigs & bombs, and The nightmare

**Date:** 2022-08-26

**Title:** Entonces en casa

**Author:** Hans Fallada

**Themes:** Home, Memory, Past

## Synopsis: Entonces en casa — Hans Fallada

Entonces en casa says 'back then at home.' Memory of a household is the subject the words announce. No author is named, so no particular family will be moved in from another book.

The tense is retrospective. Something about that home is over, or at least far enough away to be told.

It is a book of remembrance. The rooms belong to the text.

**Date:** 2022-08-26

**Title:** Hoy en casa - Otro libro -

**Author:** Hans Fallada

**Themes:** Home, Daily, Book

## Synopsis: Hoy en casa - Otro libro - — Hans Fallada

Hoy en casa - Otro libro - says 'at home today,' and the subtitle calls it another book. Domestic life in the present, or in a present being written down, is the offer. No author is attached in the record.

A sequel-feeling is in the phrase 'another book,' a return to a house already described. The note will not invent the earlier volume's events.

Expect the scale of a household, not of a nation, unless the pages surprise you.

**Date:** 2022-08-26

**Title:** Peasants, bigwigs & bombs

**Author:** Hans Fallada

**Themes:** Village, Politics, Protest

## Synopsis: Peasants, bigwigs & bombs — Hans Fallada

Peasants, bigwigs & bombs is Fallada's novel of a provincial town where farmers, party bosses, and newspapers turn a local grievance into bombs and bargains. He knew this world as a reporter and a defendant.

Nobody is allowed a clean motive for long. The comedy of small-town vanity curdles into political danger.

It is an early, tough book about how public life actually feels in a district, not in a capital.

**Date:** 2022-08-26

**Title:** The nightmare

**Author:** Hans Fallada

**Themes:** Fear, Novel, Pressure

## Synopsis: The nightmare — Hans Fallada

The nightmare is a Hans Fallada novel whose title announces a nightmare, a mind or a household under a fear that will not stay in sleep. Inventing the apparatus of that fear would be a disservice.

His novels typically put a private dread next to rent, work, and neighbors. A reader can expect that double pressure here.

The book asks to be met on the page, where the nightmare is specified.
