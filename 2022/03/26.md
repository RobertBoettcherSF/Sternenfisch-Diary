**Themes:** Death, Passage, Mind

## Synopsis: Bardo Thodol: El libro tibetano de los muertos — W. Y. Evans

Bardo Thodol: El libro tibetano de los muertos is a language-edition of the Tibetan Book of the Dead, instructions for the interval after dying. Visions come. The teaching says they are the mind's own light, to be recognized rather than feared or worshipped.

The aim is liberation, or, failing that, a less blinded rebirth. The voice is practical about a metaphysical event.

Commentaries differ by translator. The core is a guide for the time when the ordinary self comes apart.

**Title:** Bardo Thodol: The Tibetan Book of the Dead

**Author:** Walter Evans-Wentz

**Themes:** Death, Passage, Liberation

## Synopsis: Bardo Thodol: The Tibetan Book of the Dead — Walter Evans-Wentz

Bardo Thodol: The Tibetan Book of the Dead belongs to the Tibetan Book of the Dead, a guide read for the dying and the dead through the interval between lives. The text describes visions, peaceful and wrathful, and repeats that they are projections of the mind.

The practical aim is recognition. If the dead person sees the appearances for what they are, release is possible; if not, another birth follows.

Editions and commentaries differ. The core remains a set of instructions for the hours when ordinary identity fails.

**Title:** Magellan: The man and his deed

**Author:** Stefan Zweig

**Themes:** Voyage, Obsession, Discovery

## Synopsis: Magellan: The man and his deed — Stefan Zweig

Magellan: The man and his deed follows Ferdinand Magellan's attempt to reach the Spice Islands by sailing west, and the voyage that became the first circumnavigation. Zweig treats the captain as a man consumed by a route other people called impossible.

Mutiny, hunger, a strait at the bottom of the map, and a death in the Philippines leave the ships to finish what their commander began. The achievement is told as endurance more than as triumph.

The book is about the cost of a fixed idea, and about the sailors who paid it.

**Title:** The Portrait of Dorian Gray

**Author:** Oscar Wilde

**Themes:** Beauty, Corruption, Portrait

## Synopsis: The Portrait of Dorian Gray — Oscar Wilde

The Portrait of Dorian Gray is Wilde's novel of a young man whose portrait ages and sins while his face stays perfect. Influence, a poisonous book, and a wish made lightly divide body from conscience.

The epigrams are famous. Under them the story is a moral fable about a life that refuses to show its cost.

London drawing rooms and a locked attic are the two rooms the novel keeps returning to.
