# 2022-05-11 — How Uli the servant becomes happy, The drunkard, and The Young Goedeschal

## How Uli the servant becomes happy

**Date:** 2022-05-11

**Title:** How Uli the servant becomes happy

**Author:** Jeremias Gotthelf

**Themes:** Farm, Ambition, Marriage

## Synopsis: How Uli the servant becomes happy — Jeremias Gotthelf

How Uli the servant becomes happy follows Uli, a farm servant who wants to become his own master and to deserve the happiness the title names. Gotthelf is intensely practical about wages, work, and the choice of a spouse.

Moral instruction and a real story of rural advancement run together. He believes character is visible in how a man treats animals, money, and a woman's judgment.

It is a nineteenth-century Swiss novel of self-making, stern and affectionate.

## The drunkard

**Date:** 2022-05-11

**Title:** The drunkard

**Author:** Hans Fallada

**Themes:** Alcohol, Decline, Confession

## Synopsis: The drunkard — Hans Fallada

The drunkard is Fallada's novel of a businessman who slides into drink and watches his competence, marriage, and freedom go in that order. The narration has the clarity of someone describing a trap from inside it.

Institutions, a clinic, a spell of punishment, do not supply a moral miracle. The interest is the mechanism of a decline.

Fallada writes it without romantic blur. That dryness is the book's mercy and its harshness.

## The Young Goedeschal

**Date:** 2022-05-11

**Title:** The Young Goedeschal

**Author:** Hans Fallada

**Themes:** Youth, Desire, Novel

## Synopsis: The Young Goedeschal — Hans Fallada

The Young Goedeschal is Hans Fallada's early novel of adolescence, the young Goedeschal learning desire, status, and the first adult humiliations. It is a beginning writer's book and already socially exact.

School, family, and sexual curiosity are the material. The tone has not yet found the later novels' documentary weight, but the eye for embarrassment is there.

It is a portrait of a boy becoming someone who will have to live in public.
