# 2022-04-27 — Aphorisms, Folk Tales, and The Castle

## Aphorisms

**Date:** 2022-04-27

**Title:** Aphorisms

**Author:** Franz Kafka

**Themes:** Aphorism, Faith, Paradox

## Synopsis: Aphorisms — Franz Kafka

Aphorisms collects Kafka's short reflective sentences, including the late aphorisms in which belief, the Law, and the self argue in a few lines. They are not explanations of the stories.

Each one is a small trap. A comforting reading usually fails by the last clause.

The book is for readers who want Kafka's thought without a plot to hide inside.

## Folk Tales

**Date:** 2022-04-27

**Title:** Folk Tales

**Author:** Leo Tolstoy

**Themes:** Folk Tale, Moral, Simplicity

## Synopsis: Folk Tales — Leo Tolstoy

Folk Tales belongs with Leo Tolstoy's plain tales for a wide readership, stories pared down toward a moral test. A peasant, a king, or a devil usually has to choose between a clever advantage and a costly right act.

The style is deliberately simple. Tolstoy wanted these to be told aloud, not admired as complexity.

They are short, stern, and often kinder than their severity first suggests.

## The Castle

**Date:** 2022-04-27

**Title:** The Castle

**Author:** Franz Kafka

**Themes:** Authority, Village, Access

## Synopsis: The Castle — Franz Kafka

The Castle follows K., a land surveyor who may or may not have been summoned, as he tries to reach the castle that governs a village. Officials sleep, messengers lose letters, and the village treats him as a problem to be housed.

Kafka leaves the book unfinished. The movement is not toward a solution but toward a closer and more humiliating intimacy with an authority that will not appear.

It is a novel about access: who is allowed to count as employed, known, and real.
