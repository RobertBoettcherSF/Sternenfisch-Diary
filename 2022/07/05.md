**Author:** Stefan Zweig

## Synopsis: De triomf en tragedie van Erasmus van Rotterdam — Stefan Zweig

De triomf en tragedie van Erasmus van Rotterdam is Zweig's Erasmus, the scholar who wanted Christendom repaired without a war of saints. The edition's language changes. The quarrel with fanaticism does not.

Erasmus writes, hesitates, and is overrun by people who have chosen certainty. Zweig clearly prefers the hesitation.

The book is a defense of the intellectual who will not pick up the correct weapon.

**Themes:** Youth, Ambition, Size

## Synopsis: Joven caballero - muy grande — Hans Fallada

Joven caballero - muy grande uses a joking title, a young gentleman who is 'very big,' for a Hans Fallada story of youth and self-importance. The exact comic mechanism is the book's, not something to sketch from the adjective alone.

Expect a provincial or small-city scale and a hero whose estimate of himself is part of the problem. That is Fallada's home ground.

The title smiles. The prose, if it is like his other work, will not only smile.

**Title:** Magallanes

**Author:** Stefan Zweig

**Themes:** Voyage, Will, Ocean

## Synopsis: Magallanes — Stefan Zweig

Magallanes is Zweig's Magellan in translation: a captain's fixed idea, a fleet, a strait, a death, and a voyage that continues anyway until the world has been circled.

Hardship is the texture. Glory is what other people call the result.

The narrative is swift and partisan toward stubbornness.

**Title:** Reviews 1902 - 1939

**Author:** Stefan Zweig

**Themes:** Books, Criticism, Reading

## Synopsis: Reviews 1902 - 1939 — Stefan Zweig

Reviews 1902 - 1939 collects Stefan Zweig's reviews and literary encounters across the years named in the title. The pieces are responses to books and to the writers who made them, written as a working reader rather than as a system.

Taken together they sketch a taste: European, biographical, impatient with dullness, loyal to intensity.

It is a book for someone who likes criticism that still sounds like a person talking.
