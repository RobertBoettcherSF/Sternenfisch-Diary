## Synopsis: Joseph Fouché (Oversatt): Portrett av et politisk menneske (Joseph Fouché: Portrait of a Political Man) — Stefan Zweig

Joseph Fouché (Oversatt): Portrett av et politisk menneske is Stefan Zweig's portrait of Joseph Fouché, the politician who survived every change of regime in revolutionary France. He moves from radical to minister of police, serving and outlasting the men who thought they owned him.

Zweig is less interested in a list of offices than in the temperament of a survivor: cold, useful, and never last to leave a sinking alliance. Napoleon is the great antagonist.

The book reads as a study of power without loyalty, and of how a secondary figure can steer an age by refusing to be identified with it.

**Themes:** Animals, Perception, Environment

## Synopsis: Omverden og indre verden hos dyrene (Oversatt): Biokybernetikk (The Environment and Inner World of Animals: Biocybernetics) — Jakob von Uexküll

Omverden og indre verden hos dyrene is Jakob von Uexküll's study of how animals inhabit worlds built from their own senses. A tick, a sea urchin, or a dog does not enter one shared environment. Each lives inside the cues that matter to it.

The idea of the Umwelt, a surrounding world of meaning, is the book's gift to later thought. Meaning, for Uexküll, is what a creature can perceive and answer. The subtitle names that circuit biocybernetics.

The prose is scientific and pictorial. It asks the reader to stop assuming that human sight is the measure of a place.
