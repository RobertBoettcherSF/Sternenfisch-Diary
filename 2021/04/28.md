## Back then at home: Experienced, experienced and invented

**Date:** 2021-04-28

**Title:** Back then at home: Experienced, experienced and invented

**Author:** Hans Fallada

**Themes:** Childhood, Memory, Home

## Synopsis: Back then at home: Experienced, experienced and invented — Hans Fallada

Back then at home: Experienced, experienced and invented belongs to Hans Fallada's books of home, sketches of the household he came from and the one he later made. Anecdote, not thesis, is the unit.

The charm is specific: rooms, relatives, the small operations of a German domestic life across troubled decades. He does not pretend coziness is the whole truth.

These pages are memoir in pieces, approachable, and best read as reminiscence rather than as a novel with a single crisis.

## Castellio against Calvin: or a conscience against violence

**Date:** 2021-04-28

**Title:** Castellio against Calvin: or a conscience against violence

**Author:** Stefan Zweig

**Themes:** Conscience, Tolerance, Persecution

## Synopsis: Castellio against Calvin: or a conscience against violence — Stefan Zweig

Castellio against Calvin: or a conscience against violence sets Sebastian Castellio against John Calvin after the burning of Michael Servetus. Zweig takes the side of the man who argued that killing a heretic does not defend the truth.

Geneva's discipline, the pamphlets, and the loneliness of the dissenter are the scenery. The book is a polemic in the form of history.

Its question is simple and still uncomfortable: who has the right to punish a belief.

## Marceline Desbordes-Valmore: The life of a poetess

**Date:** 2021-04-28

**Title:** Marceline Desbordes-Valmore: The life of a poetess

**Author:** Stefan Zweig

**Themes:** Poetry, Biography, Woman

## Synopsis: Marceline Desbordes-Valmore: The life of a poetess — Stefan Zweig

Marceline Desbordes-Valmore: The life of a poetess is Zweig's life of Marceline Desbordes-Valmore, the French poet whose work he helped bring to readers who had overlooked it. A woman's lyric life, hardship, and persistence are the story.

The subtitle on some editions calls it the life of a poetess. The book takes that life seriously as an artistic fact, not as a footnote to famous men.

It belongs with Zweig's recoveries of figures he thought the age had mismeasured.
