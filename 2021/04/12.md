# 2021-04-12 — Afterwards, August Rodin, Environment and inner world of the animals, and Rheinsberg

## Afterwards

**Date:** 2021-04-12

**Title:** Afterwards

**Author:** Kurt Tucholsky

**Themes:** Aftermath, Memory, Tone

## Synopsis: Afterwards — Kurt Tucholsky

Afterwards is one of Tucholsky's shorter books of reflection after the event, the mood of afterward rather than of the party itself. Exact contents vary by edition; the title's promise is the residue.

He was a master of the note that sounds casual and leaves a bruise. Expect that, not a treatise.

It belongs with the late, more shadowed side of his work.

## August Rodin

**Date:** 2021-04-12

**Title:** August Rodin

**Author:** Rainer Maria Rilke

**Themes:** Sculpture, Work, Attention

## Synopsis: August Rodin — Rainer Maria Rilke

August Rodin is Rainer Maria Rilke's writing on Auguste Rodin. He watches a sculptor whose whole method is work, patience, and the surface of a body understood as a life.

The essay is also a young poet finding a discipline. Rodin's studio becomes a lesson in staying with the thing made.

It is criticism that wants to be an apprenticeship.

## Environment and inner world of the animals

**Date:** 2021-04-12

**Title:** Environment and inner world of the animals

**Author:** Jakob Johann von Uexküll

**Themes:** Animals, Perception, Environment

## Synopsis: Environment and inner world of the animals — Jakob Johann von Uexküll

Environment and inner world of the animals is Jakob von Uexküll's study of how animals inhabit worlds built from their own senses. A tick, a sea urchin, or a dog does not enter 'the' environment; each lives inside the cues that matter to it.

The argument changed biology and later philosophy. Meaning, for Uexküll, is what a creature can perceive and answer.

The prose is scientific and pictorial. It asks the reader to stop assuming that human sight is the measure of a place.

## Rheinsberg

**Date:** 2021-04-12

**Title:** Rheinsberg

**Author:** Kurt Tucholsky

**Themes:** Love, Weekend, Youth

## Synopsis: Rheinsberg — Kurt Tucholsky

Rheinsberg is Kurt Tucholsky's light story of a young couple who steal a weekend in Rheinsberg and play at being more grown than they are. The book is small on purpose.

Flirtation, a hotel, and the pleasure of a borrowed sophistication make the comedy. Under it is real affection.

It has stayed popular because it is happy without being stupid about happiness.
