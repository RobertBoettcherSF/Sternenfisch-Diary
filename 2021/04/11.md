## Synopsis: Environment and inner world of the animals — Jakob Johann von Uexküll

Environment and inner world of the animals is Jakob von Uexküll's study of how animals inhabit worlds built from their own senses. A tick, a sea urchin, or a dog does not enter 'the' environment; each lives inside the cues that matter to it.

The argument changed biology and later philosophy. Meaning, for Uexküll, is what a creature can perceive and answer.

The prose is scientific and pictorial. It asks the reader to stop assuming that human sight is the measure of a place.
