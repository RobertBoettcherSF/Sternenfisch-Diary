**Date:** 2021-04-27

**Title:** Anton and Gerda

**Author:** Hans Fallada

**Themes:** Youth, Pair, Province

## Synopsis: Anton and Gerda — Hans Fallada

Anton and Gerda names Anton and Gerda, and it is one of Hans Fallada's early novels of a young pair in a provincial world. Public detail of the plot is thinner than for his famous later books, so this note will not invent episodes.

What can be said is the scale: two young people, a German town, and Fallada's habit of watching money and affection in the same scene.

Readers who know Little Man, What Now? will recognize the human size, in an earlier key.

**Title:** Back then at home

**Author:** Hans Fallada

**Themes:** Childhood, Memory, Home

## Synopsis: Back then at home — Hans Fallada

Back then at home belongs to Hans Fallada's books of home, sketches of the household he came from and the one he later made. Anecdote, not thesis, is the unit.

The charm is specific: rooms, relatives, the small operations of a German domestic life across troubled decades. He does not pretend coziness is the whole truth.

These pages are memoir in pieces, approachable, and best read as reminiscence rather than as a novel with a single crisis.

**Title:** Brazil

**Author:** Stefan Zweig

**Themes:** Exile, Future, Country

## Synopsis: Brazil — Stefan Zweig

Brazil is Stefan Zweig's essay on Brazil, written after he had left Europe. He presents the country as a possible future, a place not yet organized around the hatreds he was fleeing.

The portrait is hopeful and incomplete, the view of a newcomer who wants to believe in what he has found.

It belongs with the last part of his life, beside the memoir of the world he had lost.

**Title:** Castellio against Calvin

**Author:** Stefan Zweig

**Themes:** Conscience, Tolerance, Persecution

## Synopsis: Castellio against Calvin — Stefan Zweig

Castellio against Calvin sets Sebastian Castellio against John Calvin after the burning of Michael Servetus. Zweig takes the side of the man who argued that killing a heretic does not defend the truth.

Geneva's discipline, the pamphlets, and the loneliness of the dissenter are the scenery. The book is a polemic in the form of history.

Its question is simple and still uncomfortable: who has the right to punish a belief.

**Title:** Marceline Desbordes-Valmore

**Author:** Stefan Zweig

**Themes:** Poetry, Biography, Woman

## Synopsis: Marceline Desbordes-Valmore — Stefan Zweig

Marceline Desbordes-Valmore is Zweig's life of Marceline Desbordes-Valmore, the French poet whose work he helped bring to readers who had overlooked it. A woman's lyric life, hardship, and persistence are the story.

The subtitle on some editions calls it the life of a poetess. The book takes that life seriously as an artistic fact, not as a footnote to famous men.

It belongs with Zweig's recoveries of figures he thought the age had mismeasured.
