**Title:** Hoppelpoppel - where are you?

**Author:** Hans Fallada

**Themes:** Child, Search, Name

## Synopsis: Hoppelpoppel - where are you? — Hans Fallada

Hoppelpoppel - where are you? calls for Hoppelpoppel and asks where he is. A name like that, and a question like that, belong to a children's search: someone small is missing and the book is the finding.

No author is named. No woodland map will be drawn here. The emotion is the one in the question, a call sent after a beloved nuisance.

It reads as a book to be said aloud, because the name is already a pleasure to say.

**Title:** Letter to Father

**Author:** Franz Kafka

**Themes:** Family, Authority, Writing

## Synopsis: Letter to Father — Franz Kafka

Letter to Father is Franz Kafka's long letter to his father, a reckoning he did not deliver as a conversation. He describes a childhood organized by a loud, successful man and a boy who experienced that strength as judgment.

Marriage, work, and writing all appear as attempts to answer a voice that was already in the room. The letter is precise about fear and also about the father's own history.

It is both a family document and a map of how authority gets inside a life.

**Title:** Short texts about historical personalities

**Author:** Stefan Zweig

**Themes:** History, Portrait, Essay

## Synopsis: Short texts about historical personalities — Stefan Zweig

Short texts about historical personalities collects Zweig's short texts on historical personalities, brief lives rather than archives. He looks for the gesture or the crisis that explains a public figure.

The method is the same as in his longer biographies, compressed. Sympathy and drama lead; the footnote does not.

It is a book of portraits for readers who like history as character.

**Title:** The Eight Octavo Booklets

**Author:** Franz Kafka

**Themes:** Notebooks, Writing, Thought

## Synopsis: The Eight Octavo Booklets — Franz Kafka

The Eight Octavo Booklets refers to the eight octavo notebooks Kafka kept, a mix of story openings, diary, and reflection. The pages show work in motion rather than a book he prepared for strangers.

A paragraph of fiction may break off into a private note. That discontinuity is part of what these notebooks are.

They are valuable if you want the hand behind the finished tales.

**Title:** The love of Erika Ewald

**Author:** Stefan Zweig

**Themes:** Youth, Love, Music

## Synopsis: The love of Erika Ewald — Stefan Zweig

The love of Erika Ewald is an early Stefan Zweig novella about a young woman's first serious attachment, set near the world of music. Feeling outruns experience, which is the risk the story keeps circling.

It is slighter than his later novellas and already interested in the moment when emotion becomes a fate.

Readers who know Beware of Pity will recognize the younger version of that concern.

**Title:** The Metamorphosis

**Author:** Franz Kafka

**Themes:** Transformation, Family, Work

## Synopsis: The Metamorphosis — Franz Kafka

The Metamorphosis begins when Gregor Samsa wakes as a monstrous insect and worries first about missing the train. The story stays inside the apartment: a job that owned him, a family that depended on his salary, and the body that ends that arrangement.

Care turns to disgust, and disgust to a practical decision. Kafka refuses a moral that would make the change a simple allegory.

What remains is the comedy and the dread of a household adjusting to someone it no longer wants to feed.

**Title:** The Wonders of Life

**Author:** Stefan Zweig

**Themes:** Science, Wonder, Life

## Synopsis: The Wonders of Life — Stefan Zweig

The Wonders of Life is Stefan Zweig's essayistic look at the wonders of life, curiosity turned toward the natural and the human rather than toward a single biography. Wonder, for him, is an educated emotion.

The pages belong with his cultural prose. They are not a novel and not a textbook.

A reader gets his characteristic warmth in the presence of anything intensely alive.

**Date:** 2021-04-13

**Title:** Worpswede

**Author:** Rainer Maria Rilke

**Themes:** Landscape, Artists, Colony

## Synopsis: Worpswede — Rainer Maria Rilke

Worpswede is Rilke's book on Worpswede, the artists' colony on the north-German moor. He writes about painters who tried to see a flat, huge landscape without turning it into a postcard.

Sky, peat, and the lives of the colonists are the material. The prose is early Rilke, already in love with looking.

It is an art book that is also a landscape book.
