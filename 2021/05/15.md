# 2021-05-15 — The Fan of Lady Windermere, The Nightingale and the Rose, The Portrait of Dorian Gray, and The priest and the altar boy

## The Fan of Lady Windermere

**Date:** 2021-05-15

**Title:** The Fan of Lady Windermere

**Author:** Oscar Wilde

**Themes:** Society, Reputation, Marriage

## Synopsis: The Fan of Lady Windermere — Oscar Wilde

The Fan of Lady Windermere is the comedy of Lady Windermere's fan: a young wife sure of her moral categories, a husband with a secret, and a woman with a past who refuses to be only a scandal. A fan left in the wrong room almost ruins several lives.

Wilde's epigrams do the glittering work. Under them is a serious dislike of people who enjoy condemning.

The play is a society drama that keeps discovering mercy where the drawing room expected a scene.

## The Nightingale and the Rose

**Date:** 2021-05-15

**Title:** The Nightingale and the Rose

**Author:** Oscar Wilde

**Themes:** Love, Sacrifice, Art

## Synopsis: The Nightingale and the Rose — Oscar Wilde

The Nightingale and the Rose tells of a nightingale that presses its breast against a thorn to stain a white rose red, because a student needs the flower for a girl. The sacrifice is perfect. The people it was meant for are not.

Wilde sets artistic and natural devotion against a shallow courtship. The bitterness is quiet and complete.

It is a story about a gift that the recipient has no way to understand.

## The Portrait of Dorian Gray

**Date:** 2021-05-15

**Title:** The Portrait of Dorian Gray

**Author:** Oscar Wilde

**Themes:** Beauty, Corruption, Portrait

## Synopsis: The Portrait of Dorian Gray — Oscar Wilde

The Portrait of Dorian Gray is Wilde's novel of a young man whose portrait ages and sins while his face stays perfect. Influence, a poisonous book, and a wish made lightly divide body from conscience.

The epigrams are famous. Under them the story is a moral fable about a life that refuses to show its cost.

London drawing rooms and a locked attic are the two rooms the novel keeps returning to.

**Title:** The priest and the altar boy

**Author:** Oscar Wilde

**Themes:** Faith, Youth, Secrecy

## Synopsis: The priest and the altar boy — Oscar Wilde

The priest and the altar boy names a priest and an altar boy, and the edition credits Oscar Wilde. Public discussion of a story under this title is tangled, and the plot is not one I will invent or assign with confidence.

What the title itself places in front of a reader is the closed world of a church: ritual, youth, authority, and a relationship the institution would rather not examine.

The note stays with that frame. A browser should treat the pages, not a rumor about them, as the text.
