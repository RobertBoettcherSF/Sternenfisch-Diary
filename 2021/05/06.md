## ... quite different - Picking your own bread makes you fat

**Date:** 2021-05-06

**Title:** ... quite different - Picking your own bread makes you fat

**Author:** Kurt Tucholsky

**Themes:** Appetite, Proverb, Comedy

## Synopsis: ... quite different - Picking your own bread makes you fat — Kurt Tucholsky

... quite different - Picking your own bread makes you fat turns a homely warning into a book: pick your own bread and you will eat too much of it. The joke is about abundance in the hand and the loss of measure.

No author is named, and no household will be invented around the loaf. The proverb is the idea a reader can trust before opening the pages.

It sounds like a comic moral tale. The title is already laughing.

## Fragments

**Date:** 2021-05-06

**Title:** Fragments

**Author:** Franz Kafka

**Themes:** Fragments, Notebooks, Aphorism

## Synopsis: Fragments — Franz Kafka

Fragments draws on Kafka's notebooks and unfinished pages. These are not polished tales so much as openings, refusals, and sentences that stop when the thought has shown its edge.

The interest is the workshop: how a legal or family dread finds a first image.

They reward slow reading, a page or two at a time.

## Joseph Fouché

**Date:** 2021-05-06

**Title:** Joseph Fouché

**Author:** Stefan Zweig

**Themes:** Power, Betrayal, Politics

## Synopsis: Joseph Fouché — Stefan Zweig

Joseph Fouché is Stefan Zweig's portrait of Joseph Fouché, the politician who survived every change of regime in revolutionary France. He moves from radical to minister of police, serving and outlasting the men who thought they owned him.

Zweig is less interested in a list of offices than in the temperament of a survivor: cold, useful, and never last to leave a sinking alliance. Napoleon is the great antagonist, not the hero.

The book reads as a study of power without loyalty, and of how a secondary figure can steer an age by refusing to be identified with it.

## Little man - What now?

**Date:** 2021-05-06

**Title:** Little man - What now?

**Author:** Hans Fallada

**Themes:** Marriage, Unemployment, City

## Synopsis: Little man - What now? — Hans Fallada

Little man - What now? is Hans Fallada's novel of a young couple trying to stay decent in a city that will not employ them. A pregnancy, a thin wage, and the relatives who judge them turn a love story into an economic one.

Fallada's gift is the price of a sausage and the tone of a landlord. Politics sits in the kitchen.

The title's question, what now, is practical. The book refuses a glamorous answer.

## Of Art-Things

**Date:** 2021-05-06

**Title:** Of Art-Things

**Author:** Rainer Maria Rilke

**Themes:** Art, Essays, Looking

## Synopsis: Of Art-Things — Rainer Maria Rilke

Of Art-Things gathers Rilke's writings on art and on the things art attends to. The pieces are less a system than a series of visits: a picture, a place, a maker, described until the looking becomes an ethic.

He trusts attention more than opinion. That trust is the argument.

The book suits readers who want criticism slow enough to resemble prayer or work.

## The Young Goedeschal

**Date:** 2021-05-06

**Title:** The Young Goedeschal

**Author:** Hans Fallada

**Themes:** Youth, Desire, Novel

## Synopsis: The Young Goedeschal — Hans Fallada

The Young Goedeschal is Hans Fallada's early novel of adolescence, the young Goedeschal learning desire, status, and the first adult humiliations. It is a beginning writer's book and already socially exact.

School, family, and sexual curiosity are the material. The tone has not yet found the later novels' documentary weight, but the eye for embarrassment is there.

It is a portrait of a boy becoming someone who will have to live in public.
