# 2021-03-27 — America, The Castle, and The Trial

**Date:** 2021-03-27

**Title:** America

**Author:** Franz Kafka

**Themes:** America, Youth, Exile

## Synopsis: America — Franz Kafka

America is Franz Kafka's unfinished novel of a European boy pushed out to America. Karl Rossmann arrives already in disgrace and moves through a country of hotels, elevators, and employers who rename him.

The book breaks off before a settled ending. What exists is a comic and anxious tour of work, patronage, and the feeling of being permanently new.

It is the lightest of Kafka's novels in scenery and still a story about not being let in.

**Date:** 2021-03-27

**Title:** The Castle

**Author:** Franz Kafka

**Themes:** Authority, Village, Access

## Synopsis: The Castle — Franz Kafka

The Castle follows K., a land surveyor who may or may not have been summoned, as he tries to reach the castle that governs a village. Officials sleep, messengers lose letters, and the village treats him as a problem to be housed.

Kafka leaves the book unfinished. The movement is not toward a solution but toward a closer and more humiliating intimacy with an authority that will not appear.

It is a novel about access: who is allowed to count as employed, known, and real.

**Date:** 2021-03-27

**Title:** The Trial

**Author:** Franz Kafka

**Themes:** Law, Guilt, Bureaucracy

## Synopsis: The Trial — Franz Kafka

The Trial opens with an arrest that names no charge. Josef K. spends the rest of the book trying to find the court that has claimed him, in attics, stairwells, and conversations that explain nothing usable.

Every attempt to be reasonable feeds the procedure. Helpers, women, a painter, and a priest offer interpretations that do not free him.

The novel is about a guilt that arrives before the reason, and about offices that exist to prolong the case.
