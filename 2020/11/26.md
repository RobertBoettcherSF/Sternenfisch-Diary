**Title:** How the Steffelbauer was not robbed

**Themes:** Farm, Robbery, Surprise

## Synopsis: How the Steffelbauer was not robbed

How the Steffelbauer was not robbed tells the outcome in its title: a farmer, the Steffelbauer, keeps what someone meant to take. The pleasure is a theft that does not come off.

The materials are rural and comic. A name, a farm, and an expectation of robbery are enough to carry a tale about luck, cunning, or simple stubbornness.

It is the kind of story that smiles because the disaster stays outside.

**Title:** Marriages are made in heaven

**Author:** Franz Kuenlin

**Themes:** Marriage, Proverb, Fate

## Synopsis: Marriages are made in heaven — Franz Kuenlin

Marriages are made in heaven takes the old saying that a match is appointed, not merely negotiated, and gives it to Franz Kuenlin as a whole book. Courtship and family decision sit under a proverb that is half a theology and half a joke.

The interest is the hope that affection and circumstance were already in agreement before the couple knew it. Heaven, in a title like this, is a way of talking about fate.

Readers of marriage stories will recognize the question: who, in the end, is allowed to choose.

**Title:** The pierced barrel

**Author:** Daniel Ernst Bornschein

**Themes:** Comedy, Waste, Tale

## Synopsis: The pierced barrel — Daniel Ernst Bornschein

The pierced barrel is Daniel Ernst Bornschein's tale built on a simple object: a barrel that cannot keep what is poured into it. A leak is a comic and a moral image, waste you can point at.

Popular storytelling of his time liked to hang a lesson on something a household would recognize. The barrel is that something.

It is a short tale, concrete and small in scale, not a novel of society.

**Title:** The Senator

**Author:** Andrej Belyj

**Themes:** Power, City, Bureaucracy

## Synopsis: The Senator — Andrej Belyj

The Senator places a high official at the center and gives the book to Andrei Bely, the Russian modernist who could make a rank feel like a fever. Offices, avenues, and private dread share a climate in his prose.

A senator is power in a frock coat: protocol, fear of disorder, and a city that does not stay politely realistic. Bely's sentences are part of the portrait.

The book is for a reader who wants a man of the state seen from the inside of a style, not a neutral biography.

**Date:** 2020-11-26

**Title:** The Indian Hut

**Author:** Bernardin de Saint-Pierre

**Themes:** Wisdom, Exile, Search

## Synopsis: The Indian Hut — Bernardin de Saint-Pierre

The Indian Hut is Bernardin de Saint-Pierre's philosophical tale of a scholar who travels in search of truth and finds it, not in a library, but in conversation with an outcaste living in a simple hut. The journey mocks the pride of learned systems.

Nature, equality, and the dignity of a person the caste order has pushed aside are the pressures of the story. The hut is an argument.

It is a late-eighteenth-century conte, earnest and picturesque, about where wisdom is supposed to live.
